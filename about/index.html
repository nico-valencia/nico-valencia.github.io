About

I am a geophysicist interested in understanding Earth's interior structure and dynamic processes through seismic waves. My research combines aspects of theoretical and observational seismology to extract more information from geophysical datasets.

In 2026, I start my PhD at UC Santa Barbara, where I will work on a brand new OBS dataset from the Galapagos triple junction. I plan on applying a slew of seismic techniques (body wave tomography, attenuation, receiver functions, shear-wave splitting, etc) in order to better understand mid-ocean ridge structure, dynamics, and evolution. This is the densest OBS experiment on a mid-ocean ridge in decades, and research will shed light on these fundamental tectonic features!

My undergraduate research at UC Berkeley focused on measuring azimuthal anisotropy in the southwest Pacific using shear-wave splitting measurements from SKS and direct S phases. I also worked on Ps receiver function analysis for the Antarctic ice sheet during an NSF REU Fellowship at Brown University. Additionally, I have been employed as a Data Science R&D Intern at SoundThinking for the SafePointe platform, working on magnetic time series analysis, physics simulations, and sensor development.
